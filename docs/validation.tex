\section{Scope and methodology}
\label{sec:philosophy}
%=====================================================================
This document is the agreement audit for \texttt{pyMack}, the compressible linear-stability solver
whose formulation and numerics are developed in the companion documents (\emph{The Mean
Boundary-Layer Profile}, \emph{Derivation of the Disturbance Equations}, \emph{Numerical Methods}).
Each case below (i) reruns a published or external reference at its \emph{exact} stated conditions,
(ii) measures agreement against the digitised or tabulated reference, and (iii) records a verdict on
a fixed three-tier scale applied uniformly to every case,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verdict}
  e\le5\,\%\ \Rightarrow\ \textbf{agrees},\qquad
  5\,\%<e\le15\,\%\ \Rightarrow\ \textbf{acceptable},\qquad
  e>15\,\%\ \Rightarrow\ \textbf{disagrees},
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the case's headline relative error; dimensional cases use the mean absolute error as a
fraction of the curve span. Nothing is tuned to pass, and the disagreements are kept in full view.

\paragraph{Organisation: an evidence pyramid.} The cases are ordered from the most rigorously
anchored physics to the engineering configurations, each tier earning trust only once the tier
beneath it holds:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Incompressible core.} The Orr--Sommerfeld temporal eigenvalue for plane Poiseuille
flow \cite{orszag1971} is a scalar with a textbook-exact answer. Matching it to $\sim\!10^{-5}$
confirms the spectral discretisation, the eigenvalue assembly, and the boundary-condition
enforcement in isolation, before any compressible physics is added.
\item \textbf{Compressible eigenvalue anchors.} Malik's tabulated compressible eigenvalues
\cite{malik1990} and the Balakumar--Malik discrete/continuous-spectrum topology
\cite{balakumar1992} pin the compressible operator to published digits at single
$(R,\omega)$ stations; these are the sharpest possible tests, free of digitisation error.
\item \textbf{Curve families.} Mack's neutral curves and growth-rate families \cite{mack1984}, the
Ma--Zhong $M{=}4.5$ neutral curve \cite{mazhong2003}, and the \"Ozgen--K{\i}rcal{\i} Fig.~3
neutral-curve family \cite{ozgen2008} test full \emph{curves} over Reynolds number and Mach number
and test topology as well as magnitude. They are reported by physical mode: second mode
(Section~\ref{sec:secondmode}), first mode (Section~\ref{sec:firstmode}), and the \"Ozgen family,
whose panels span \emph{both} modes, separately (Section~\ref{sec:mixedmode}).
\item \textbf{Engineering and open cases.} A Mach-6 flat plate \cite{egorov2006} anchors the second
mode on a flight-relevant configuration. The remaining engineering cases (a sharp-cone
$N$-factor, an independent collaborator's Mach 5.35 code, and the spatial $N$-factor pipeline)
carry unfinished elements and, together with the $M\approx2.2$ first-mode disagreements, are
collected in the companion note \texttt{work\_in\_progress.tex}.
\end{enumerate}
The base of the pyramid is exact; the middle is a mix of exact (second mode) and partial (first
mode). The second-mode design target is trustworthy throughout; the first mode is usable with the
documented caveats.

\paragraph{Provenance and reproducibility.} All figures embedded here are \texttt{pyMack}'s own
overlays, \texttt{pyMack} curves plotted over digitised reference \emph{points}, so no
copyrighted reference figure is reproduced. Every number and verdict is taken verbatim from the
committed per-case \texttt{verdict.json} files under \texttt{verification/} and the repository
success matrix, which are regenerated by the scripts recorded in each case directory. This document
reports only the settled validations; the open items are tracked in
\texttt{work\_in\_progress.tex}.

\subsection{Orszag (1971): plane-Poiseuille Orr--Sommerfeld eigenvalue}
\label{sec:orszag}

\paragraph{Reference.} Orszag \cite{orszag1971}, the canonical plane-Poiseuille temporal
eigenvalue.

\paragraph{Conditions.} Plane Poiseuille flow $U=1-y^2$ on $[-1,1]$, Reynolds number $Re=10\,000$,
streamwise wavenumber $\alpha=1$, temporal formulation.

\paragraph{Quantity.} The least-stable eigenvalue (phase speed) $c=c_r+\imag c_i$.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{agrees}.} \texttt{pyMack}'s Chebyshev Orr--Sommerfeld solver
(\texttt{validation/test\_orr\_sommerfeld.py}) reproduces the benchmark
$c=0.23753+0.00374\,\imag$ to an absolute error below $10^{-4}$ already at $N=64$, tightening
toward $\sim\!10^{-7}$ under refinement (Table~\ref{tab:orszag}). Because this is a scalar
comparison against an essentially exact reference rather than a hand-digitised curve, no overlay
figure is warranted. The same test module also recovers the Blasius Tollmien--Schlichting mode at
$Re_{\delta^\ast}=998$, $\alpha=0.179$ ($c_r\approx0.36$, unstable) and finds unstable growth at
$Re=1000$, confirming the mapped semi-infinite domain and the growth-rate optimisation loop; these
are sign/range assertions rather than digit-exact anchors and are not tabulated separately.

\subsection{Orszag (1971) Table~5: the full Orr--Sommerfeld spectrum}
\label{sec:orszag-spectrum}

\paragraph{Reference.} Orszag \cite{orszag1971}, Table~5: the 32 least-stable Orr--Sommerfeld
eigenvalues of plane Poiseuille flow at $\alpha=1$, $R=10\,000$, each classified symmetric ($S$) or
antisymmetric ($A$).

\paragraph{Quantity.} The full temporal eigenvalue \emph{spectrum}: not only the single
least-stable mode of Section~\ref{sec:orszag}, but the whole branch structure.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{agrees}.} \texttt{pyMack}'s dense eigensolver
(\texttt{validation/test\_orszag\_full\_spectrum.py}) reproduces \emph{all 32} tabulated
eigenvalues to a maximum absolute error $7\times10^{-6}$ (median $5\times10^{-8}$); $32/32$ match
to $<10^{-5}$ at $N=128$. It resolves the A-branch ($c_r\!\sim\!0.96$ downward), the P-branch
($c_r\!\sim\!0.2$--$0.5$), and the vertical S-branch, including the near-degenerate $S$/$A$ pairs
that differ only in the fifth--sixth digit (e.g.\ modes 2 and 3 at $0.96463$ vs.\ $0.96464$). This
upgrades the incompressible-core anchor from a single eigenvalue to the full branch structure and
exercises the dense full-spectrum path (Figure~\ref{fig:orszag-spectrum}).

\subsection{Malik (1990): compressible eigenvalue anchors}
\label{sec:malik}

\paragraph{Reference.} Malik \cite{malik1990}, the standard tabulated compressible-LST
test-case suite. Case~6 (Table~IX, the $M=4.5$ spatial second mode) is the repository's
long-standing anchor, cross-checked digit-for-digit against Xi, Ren \& Fu \cite{xirenfu2020} and
Hildebrand et al.\ \cite{hildebrand2018}. Cases 3, 4, 5 (Tables~IV/V/VI) and the grid-refinement
case of Table~X had been logged non-reproducible for want of their dimensional conditions
(\cite{hildebrand2018} lists only $M$ and $R$), until Malik's own Table~I supplied the missing
$T_0$ and wall temperatures (in particular case~4's \emph{cooled} wall $T_w/T_{\rm adb}=0.1$, which
earlier attempts had assumed adiabatic), unlocking all four.

\paragraph{Conditions.} All cases share $Pr=0.7$, $\gamma=1.4$, Sutherland $S=110.33$~K, $L^\ast$
length scale. The suite spans $M=2.5$--$10$, temporal (fixed real $\alpha$, complex $\omega$) and
spatial (fixed real $\omega$, complex $\alpha$) formulations, and adiabatic and cooled walls.
Case~3 ($M=2.5$, oblique $\beta=0.1$) is a first mode; the rest are second (Mack) modes.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{agrees} (4 of 5), \emph{acceptable} (Table X).}
Table~\ref{tab:malik}. Every real part ($\alpha_r$ or $\omega_r$) is essentially exact
($\le0.14\,\%$), fixing each mode and its phase speed; four of the five \emph{agree}. The one
\emph{acceptable} is Table~X's \emph{spatial} $\alpha_i$ ($11.5\,\%$), which is exactly the documented
hypersonic-second-mode inter-method spread (Tumin's recomputation of case~6's $\alpha_i$ from
Malik's printed value differs by $\sim\!11\,\%$ in the same way, and the solver of
\cite{xirenfu2020} gives $-0.002780$). Notably the \emph{temporal} $M=10$ growth rates (cases 4, 5)
match to $\sim\!2\,\%$, so the spatial $\alpha_i$ is the more formulation-sensitive quantity.
Case~5 is Malik's deliberately near-neutral ``severe test'': his own SDSP scheme flips $\omega_i$
negative at coarse resolution, yet \texttt{pyMack} lands on his converged value
(Figure~\ref{fig:malik5}).

\subsection{Malik (1990) Fig.~4: M10 second-mode eigenfunction}
\label{sec:malik-fig4}

\paragraph{Reference.} Malik \cite{malik1990}, Fig.~4: the disturbance eigenfunctions of the same
$M=10$ cooled-wall condition as case~4. The comparison uses the temperature eigenfunction
$\hat T$ (real and imaginary parts), the dominant, cleanly traceable curves; the small velocity
and pressure eigenfunctions overlap near the axis and are not reliably digitisable, so they are
omitted.

\paragraph{Quantity.} The eigen\emph{vector} structure: every other anchor in this document
checks eigen\emph{values}; this is the eigenfunction (mode-shape) check. The complex temperature
eigenfunction $\hat T(y)$, normalised so that $\hat T_r$ peaks at $+1$ (Malik's convention, which
fixes the eigenvector's single global phase).

\paragraph{Result: \emph{agrees}.} Both the real and imaginary temperature eigenfunctions lie
on the digitised Fig.~4 points (Figure~\ref{fig:malik-fig4}). \texttt{pyMack}'s $\hat T_r$ traces
the near-wall negative lobe, the zero crossing near $y\!\approx\!6$, and the sharp unit peak at
$y=13.3$ ($\approx\delta^\ast$) against Malik's $\approx13$ (a $2.3\,\%$ location match, RMS $0.05$
over $0\!<\!y\!<\!20$). \texttt{pyMack}'s $\hat T_i$ reproduces the same double-lobed shape,
a positive hump ($\approx0.45$) near $y\!\approx\!11$ and a negative dip ($\approx-0.5$) near
$y\!\approx\!15$, to RMS $0.08$. Under the \emph{single} global phase that makes $\hat T_r$ real
at its peak, \emph{both} parts agree, so the temperature eigenvector is validated in full, not
merely its peak location. The temperature fluctuation dominates the velocity
($|\hat T|/|\hat u|\approx14$), the hallmark of the hypersonic Mack mode.

\subsection{Balakumar \& Malik (1992): discrete/continuous spectrum topology}
\label{sec:bm1992}

\paragraph{Reference.} Balakumar \& Malik \cite{balakumar1992}. The paper presents its branches
graphically; no tabulated branch eigenvalues are openly recoverable, so this is a qualitative
branch-topology check plus a fully specified spatial second-mode cross-check.

\paragraph{Conditions.} $M=4.5$, $T_0=311$~K, $Re=1000$, $\omega=0.2$, adiabatic (insulated) wall.

\paragraph{Quantity.} (a) The topology, the separation of the discrete second mode from the
continuous spectrum in the complex-$\alpha$ plane; (b) the discrete spatial eigenvalue $\alpha$.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{acceptable}.} \texttt{pyMack}'s full spatial spectrum reproduces the
published qualitative topology: the discrete second mode sits at
$\alpha=0.220210-0.002787\,\imag$ ($c=0.9082$), matching the published
$0.220-0.003091\,\imag$ ($\alpha_r$ $0.10\,\%$, $\alpha_i$ $9.8\,\%$), and is the unique
least-damped root in the second-mode phase-speed band, while a cluster of 93 roots accumulates at
the freestream phase speed $c\!\to\!1$ (the continuous spectrum). A caveat: the separation is in
the phase-speed/growth-rate sense, \emph{not} a crisp Euclidean gap: the discretised continuum
is dense in $\alpha_r$, so the nearest other eigenvalue is only $0.019$ away (separation ratio
$0.09$) and 42 roots fall within $10\,\%$ of $|\alpha_{\rm disc}|$. That is the expected
discretisation signature of the continuous spectrum crowding the discrete mode, hence
\emph{acceptable} (topology correct) rather than \emph{agrees} (which would require a clean
Euclidean gap). A companion row, \texttt{balakumar\_malik1992\_via\_xirenfu}, treats the same
condition purely as a spatial second-mode anchor: \texttt{pyMack}
$\alpha=0.2202103-0.0027906\,\imag$ gives $\alpha_r$ relative error $0.096\,\%$ (essentially
exact) and $\alpha_i$ relative error $9.7\,\%$, the same $\sim\!10\,\%$ inter-method
$\alpha_i$ spread seen on Malik case~6 (Figure~\ref{fig:bm1992}).

\subsection{Mack (1984) Fig.~10.6: second-mode growth vs.\ Reynolds number}

\paragraph{Conditions.} Air, adiabatic wall, 2-D wave ($\psi=0^\circ$), at $M=4.5$, $5.8$, $7$, and

\subsection{Egorov et al.\ (2006): Mach-6 flat-plate second mode}
\label{sec:egorov}

\paragraph{Reference.} Egorov, Fedorov \& Soudakov \cite{egorov2006}: 2-D DNS second-mode growth
over a Mach-6 adiabatic flat plate, reported to agree with LST.

\paragraph{Conditions.} Perfect gas $\gamma=1.4$, $Pr=0.72$, power-law viscosity
$\mu/\mu_e=(T/T_e)^{0.7}$; $M_\infty=6$, $Re_L=2\times10^6$, adiabatic wall; mode-S circular
frequency $\omega\approx200$ (plate-length units) at the synchronisation station $x=1$
($R=\sqrt{2\times10^6}=1414$).

\paragraph{Quantity.} The second-mode instability band and the most-amplified dimensionless
frequency. A caveat: the paper presents the DNS-vs-LST comparison as a growth-rate-vs-$x$ curve
with no printed growth-rate digit, so no $\le$5\,\% numeric anchor exists; the verdict metric is
the frequency match.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{acceptable}.} \texttt{pyMack}'s discrete second mode
($c_r\approx0.92$--$0.94$) is unstable across $\omega_E\in[140,260]$ (bracketing the reference
forcing $\omega=200$), most-amplified at $\omega_E\approx215$ ($7.5\,\%$ from $200$); at exactly
$\omega=200$ the spatial growth is $-\alpha_i=2.90\times10^{-3}$ with $c_r=0.934$.
\texttt{pyMack} thus places the M6 second-mode instability squarely in the stated band with the
amplified frequency essentially on top of the reference forcing choice (the
\texttt{egorov2006\_m6} overlay is on disk but not embedded here).

\subsection{Ma \& Zhong (2003) Fig.~15: M4.5 second-mode neutral curve}
\label{sec:mazhong}

\paragraph{Reference.} Ma \& Zhong \cite{mazhong2003}, \S6 / Fig.~15: full neutral curves
$\omega(R)$ for the first and second mode at $M=4.5$, with the second-mode branch points quoted in
text at $R=806$ (branch~I) and $R=999.6$ (branch~II) for $F=2.2\times10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{Conditions.} Air, adiabatic \emph{mean} wall, 2-D ($\psi=0^\circ$), $M=4.5$,
$T_e=65.15$~K, unit $Re=7.2\times10^6$/m, Sutherland $S=110.33$~K, $Pr=0.72$, $\gamma=1.4$;
spatial formulation at fixed $F=2.2\times10^{-4}$. The \emph{disturbance} temperature wall
condition is isothermal ($\hat{T}|_{w}=0$), which the authors state they used for the Fig.~15
neutral curve.

\paragraph{Quantity.} The full neutral curve $\omega(R)$ traced by \texttt{pyMack}'s
$-\alpha_i=0$ contour, anchored against the two second-mode branch points quoted in the text at
$F=2.2\times10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{agrees}.} \texttt{pyMack}'s second-mode neutral band reproduces the
closed loop of Fig.~15 (nose near $R\approx280$, upper branch $\omega\approx0.225$, lower branch
$\omega\approx0.18$) across the full range. At the quoted $F=2.2\times10^{-4}$ station,
\texttt{pyMack}'s band crosses at $R=786.6$ (branch~I, vs.\ $806$, $2.4\,\%$) and $R=1009.9$
(branch~II, vs.\ $999.6$, $1.0\,\%$), comfortably inside the \emph{agrees} threshold. (Using
the adiabatic disturbance condition by mistake gives $831$/$1033$, i.e.\ $3.1\,\%$/$3.4\,\%$;
switching to the paper's actual isothermal disturbance condition is a correction, not a fit, and
tightens the match.) The first-mode \emph{cutoff} (upper) branch, re-extracted with the
discrete-mode selector (freestream decay $+$ $y_{\max}$-stationarity, replacing the earlier
phase-speed-band classifier), tracks Fig.~15's upper branch to $\sim\!3.5\,\%$ median. The
low-$\omega$ \emph{onset} (lower) branch is \emph{not} recovered: below $\omega\approx0.03$ the
2-D first mode ($c_r\!\lesssim\!0.45$) delocalises into the slow-acoustic continuous spectrum
($c_r\le1-1/M=0.778$), leaving no clean discrete eigenmode. This truncation is consistent across
\texttt{pyMack}'s spatial and temporal collocation solvers and a QR-shooting variant, and a
wave-angle sweep confirms the 2-D mode is the \emph{most} unstable here (so this is a 2-D, not an
oblique, first mode). Recovering the onset branch would need a compound-matrix shooting solver
robust to the near-continuous-spectrum decay rates; it is filed as an open item in the companion
note \texttt{work\_in\_progress.tex} (Figure~\ref{fig:mazhong}).

\caption{Ma \& Zhong Fig.~15 \cite{mazhong2003}, $M=4.5$ (\emph{agrees}, on the second mode):
\texttt{pyMack}'s second-mode neutral loop (orange) over the pixel-digitised Fig.~15 points,

\section{First mode: curves and families}
\label{sec:firstmode}
%=====================================================================
The first mode was \texttt{pyMack}'s documented weak spot. After a reference-digitisation
correction, most of the Mack first-mode figures now agree or are acceptable; the remaining
disagreement is narrow and isolated to the $M\approx2.2$ cases (Fig.~10.1 M2.2 and Fig.~10.3
M2.2), a genuine under-amplification / too-high critical Reynolds number at that Mach rather than
a general trend, and is tracked in the companion note \texttt{work\_in\_progress.tex}. This
section reports the Mack first-mode figures; the \"Ozgen--K{\i}rcal{\i} neutral-curve family,
whose panels contain first-mode branches alongside second-mode ones, is treated separately in
Section~\ref{sec:mixedmode}.

\subsection{Mack (1984) Fig.~10.1: first-mode neutral loop}
\label{sec:mack101}

\paragraph{Reference.} Mack \cite{mack1984}, Fig.~10.1, the innermost (complete-equations) neutral
loop, re-digitised with both branches plus nose.

\paragraph{Conditions.} Air, adiabatic wall, 2-D ($\psi=0^\circ$), at $M=1.6$ and $M=2.2$;
temporal neutral loop ($c_i=0$) in $(R, F\!\times\!10^4)$.

\paragraph{Quantity.} The first-mode neutral loop (lower onset $+$ upper cutoff branches) and the
critical Reynolds number.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{acceptable} (M1.6).} At \textbf{M1.6}, loop-average median

the acceptable band; the neutral loop closely tracks Mack's complete-equations loop after the
corrected reference (Figure~\ref{fig:mack101_m16}). The companion $M=2.2$ loop \emph{disagrees}
(loop-average $54.5\,\%$, critical $R$ \texttt{pyMack} $\sim\!400$ vs.\ Mack $\sim\!300$; a genuine

\section{Mixed first and second mode: the \"Ozgen--K{\i}rcal{\i} neutral-curve family}
\label{sec:mixedmode}
%=====================================================================
Fig.~3 of \"Ozgen \& K{\i}rcal{\i} \cite{ozgen2008} presents, for each Mach number from 2 to 10, a
single neutral-curve figure in the $(Re_L,\alpha_L)$ plane. At $M=2$ and $3$ the figure contains
the first-mode lobe alone; from $M\approx4$ the second (Mack) mode enters, and each panel then
spans \emph{both} instability families; at $M=6$, for example, four branches: second-mode upper
and lower, first-mode cutoff, and first-mode onset. Because a single reproduced figure tests both
modes at once, these cases are classified neither under the first nor the second mode but as a
mixed family (\texttt{verification/mixed\_mode/ozgen\_fig3/}), and they are reported last, drawing
on both preceding sections. The per-panel \texttt{verdict.json} records
\texttt{mode:\,first} for M2/M3 and \texttt{mode:\,first+second} for M4--M10.

\paragraph{Reference.} \"Ozgen \& K{\i}rcal{\i} \cite{ozgen2008}, Fig.~3 (M = 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8,
10).

\paragraph{Conditions.} Air, adiabatic wall, 2-D ($\psi=0^\circ$); temporal neutral contour
($c_i=0$) in the $(Re_L,\alpha_L)$ plane.

\paragraph{Quantity.} The neutral-curve branches $\alpha_L(Re_L)$: all branches present in each
panel, first \emph{and} second mode.

\paragraph{Result: \emph{agrees}/\emph{acceptable}.} After the references were re-digitised
correctly and a discrete-mode extractor (eigenfunction decay $+$ $y_{\max}$-stationarity, the two
acceptance tests of the companion \emph{Numerical Methods} document) replaced the earlier
$c_r$-band classifier that had excluded the genuine first mode, the picture is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{M4: agrees} (Figure~\ref{fig:ozgen_m4}). Complete two-lobe match: both
second-mode branches at the digitisation floor ($0\,\%$ upper / $0\,\%$ lower), first-mode cutoff
$2\,\%$ over $\sim\!85\,\%$ coverage; only the deep low-$\alpha$ first-mode onset tracks at
$32\,\%$.
\item \textbf{M2, M3, M6: acceptable} (Figures~\ref{fig:ozgen_m2} and \ref{fig:ozgen_m6}). M2
traces both first-mode branches over the full $R$-range by eigenvalue continuation (lower
$7\,\%$, upper $8\,\%$); M3 first-mode cutoff $6\,\%$ where resolvable (onset
continuous-spectrum-blocked); M6 four branches with second-mode upper $2\,\%$, lower $3\,\%$,
first-mode cutoff $9\,\%$, first-mode onset $50\,\%$.
\item \textbf{M7, M8, M10: acceptable (topology caveat).} The dominant second-mode cutoff
branch agrees ($2$--$6\,\%$), but \texttt{pyMack} predicts a \emph{connected}
first--second-mode band where the reference shows two lobes separated by a stable gap. This is a
documented connected-vs-separated topology difference (inter-mode over-prediction at high Mach),
not a second-mode-curve error.
\end{itemize}
A separate growth-contour case \texttt{ozgen\_fig3\_lobes} \textbf{disagrees} ($c_i=0.004$ lobe
median $18.8\,\%$, and the interior $c_i=0.012$ M2 lobe is absent because \texttt{pyMack}'s peak
M2 growth $c_{i,\max}=0.0109<0.012$), which is the same open-lobe / under-amplification
discrepancy. A
combined all-Mach overlay exists at \texttt{figures/ozgen\_fig3\_overlay.png}. To hold the page
count, only M2, M4, and M6 are embedded below; the M3/M7/M8/M10 overlays exist under
\texttt{verification/\allowbreak mixed\_mode/\allowbreak ozgen\_fig3/\allowbreak M*} and are
covered by the tabulated verdicts.
