\begin{abstract}
This note is the honest agreement audit for \textbf{pyMack}, the compressible
linear-stability solver. Each case runs a published or external reference at its
\emph{exact} stated conditions, measures agreement against the digitised
reference, and reports a verdict on a fixed three-tier scale
(\textbf{agrees}~$\le$\,5\,\%, \textbf{acceptable}~5--15\,\%,
\textbf{disagrees} otherwise). Nothing here is tuned to pass. The picture is
deliberately two-sided: pyMack's design target --- the compressible
\emph{second} (Mack) mode --- agrees or is acceptable in every case, across
five independent sources, Mach 4.5--10, and a flight-relevant cone; the
\emph{first} mode agrees with \"Ozgen \& K\i rcal\i\ where the discrete mode is
cleanly resolvable but remains the documented weak spot at low Mach and on the
Mack first-mode figures. Every figure embedded here is pyMack's own overlay ---
pyMack curves plotted over digitised reference \emph{points} --- so no
copyrighted reference figure is reproduced. All figures, numbers, and verdicts
are taken verbatim from the committed per-case \texttt{verdict.json} files and
the repository success matrix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Validation philosophy: a pyramid}
\label{sec:philosophy}

\problembox{Before trusting a stability prediction on a real geometry, we must
know \emph{where} the solver is exact, where it is merely acceptable, and where
it fails. This note answers that question case by case, keeping the
disagreements in full view.}

We organise the evidence as a pyramid, from the most rigorously anchored physics
at the base to the engineering cases at the top. Each tier only earns trust once
the tier beneath it holds.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Incompressible core.} The Orr--Sommerfeld temporal eigenvalue for
plane Poiseuille flow is a scalar with a textbook-exact answer. Matching it to
$\sim\!10^{-5}$ confirms the spectral discretisation, the eigenvalue assembly,
and the boundary-condition enforcement in isolation, before any compressible
physics is added.
\item \textbf{Compressible eigenvalue anchors.} Malik's (1990) tabulated
compressible spatial eigenvalues, and the Balakumar \& Malik (1992) discrete/
continuous-spectrum topology, pin the compressible operator to published digits
at a single $(R,\omega)$ station --- the sharpest possible test, free of
digitisation error.
\item \textbf{Second-mode and first-mode benchmarks.} Mack's (1984) AGARD-R-709
neutral curves and growth-rate families, and \"Ozgen \& K\i rcal\i's (2008)
neutral curves, test full \emph{curves} over Reynolds number and Mach number ---
topology as well as magnitude.
\item \textbf{Engineering cases.} A sharp cone (Sivasubramanian 2012), an
independent collaborator's Mach 5.35 nitrogen code, a Mach-6 flat plate
(Egorov 2006), and spatial $N$-factor integration exercise the whole pipeline on
flight-relevant configurations.
\end{enumerate}

\plain{The base of the pyramid is exact. The middle is a mix of exact
(second mode) and partial (first mode). The top inherits both: the second-mode
engineering predictions are trustworthy; the first mode should be used with the
documented caveats.}

\subsection{Orszag (1971) plane-Poiseuille Orr--Sommerfeld eigenvalue}
\label{sec:orszag}

\textbf{Reference source.} S.~A. Orszag, ``Accurate solution of the
Orr--Sommerfeld stability equation'' (1971) --- the canonical plane-Poiseuille
temporal eigenvalue.

\textbf{Flow conditions.} Plane Poiseuille flow $U=1-y^2$ on $[-1,1]$, Reynolds
number $Re=10\,000$, streamwise wavenumber $\alpha=1$, temporal formulation.

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The least-stable eigenvalue (phase speed)
$c=c_r+\imag c_i$.

\textbf{Result.} pyMack's Chebyshev Orr--Sommerfeld solver
(\texttt{validation/test\_orr\_sommerfeld.py}) reproduces Orszag's benchmark
$c=0.23753+0.00374\,\imag$ to an absolute error below $10^{-4}$ already at
$N=64$, tightening toward $\sim\!10^{-7}$ under refinement
(Table~\ref{tab:orszag}). Because this is a scalar comparison against an
essentially exact reference --- not a hand-digitised curve --- no overlay figure
is warranted.

\section{Compressible eigenvalue anchors}

\subsection{Malik (1990) case 6 --- compressible spatial eigenvalue}
\label{sec:malik6}

\textbf{Reference source.} M.~R. Malik, ``Numerical methods for hypersonic
boundary layer stability'' (1990) \emph{J.\ Comput.\ Phys.} \textbf{86}:376,
Table~IX, Case~6. The published $\alpha$ is cross-checked digit-for-digit against
Xi, Ren \& Fu (arXiv:2006.05970, Table~3) and to 4--5 digits against Hildebrand
et al.\ (arXiv:1712.08239, Table~I).

\textbf{Flow conditions.} $M_e=4.5$, $R=1500$, $\omega=0.23$, $\beta=0$,
$Pr=0.7$, $\gamma=1.4$, adiabatic (insulated) wall; $T_0=611.11$~K,
$T_e\approx121.0$~K, Sutherland $S=110.33$~K; $L^\ast$ length scale.

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The spatial streamwise eigenvalue
$\alpha=\alpha_r+\imag\alpha_i$ of the discrete second (Mack) mode.

\textbf{Result --- \emph{agrees}.} pyMack gives
$\alpha=0.2533998-0.0024898\,\imag$ against Malik's $0.2534048-0.0024921\,\imag$:
$\alpha_r$ relative error $1.95\times10^{-5}$ ($0.002\,\%$, essentially exact),
$\alpha_i$ relative error $9.16\times10^{-4}$ ($0.09\,\%$), phase speed
$c=0.9077$ ($N=120$). The $\sim\!0.1\,\%$ $\alpha_i$ deviation is far inside the
published inter-method spread for this case (Tumin 2007 recomputes
$\alpha_i=-0.0027738$, $\sim\!11\,\%$ from Malik; Xi/Ren/Fu's own solver gives
$-0.002780$). This is the repository's Layer-4b CI gate (Figure~\ref{fig:malik6}).

\subsection{Balakumar \& Malik (1992) --- discrete/continuous spectrum topology}
\label{sec:bm1992}

\textbf{Reference source.} P.~Balakumar \& M.~R. Malik, ``Discrete modes and
continuous spectra in supersonic boundary layers'' (1992) \emph{J.\ Fluid Mech.}
\textbf{239}:631--656. The paper presents its branches graphically; no tabulated
branch eigenvalues are openly recoverable, so this is a qualitative branch-
topology check plus a fully-specified spatial second-mode cross-check.

\textbf{Flow conditions.} $M=4.5$, $T_0=311$~K, $Re=1000$, $\omega=0.2$,
adiabatic (insulated) wall.

\textbf{Quantity compared.} (a) the topology --- separation of the discrete
second mode from the continuous spectrum in the complex-$\alpha$ plane; (b) the
discrete spatial eigenvalue $\alpha$.

\textbf{Result --- \emph{acceptable}.} pyMack's full spatial spectrum reproduces
B\&M's qualitative topology: the discrete second mode sits at
$\alpha=0.220210-0.002787\,\imag$ ($c=0.9082$) --- matching the published
$0.220-0.003091\,\imag$ ($\alpha_r$ $0.10\,\%$, $\alpha_i$ $9.8\,\%$) --- and is
the unique least-damped root in the second-mode phase-speed band, while a cluster
of 93 roots accumulates at the freestream phase speed $c\!\to\!1$ (the continuous
spectrum). \textbf{Honest caveat:} the separation is in the phase-speed/growth-
rate sense, \emph{not} a crisp Euclidean gap --- the discretised continuum is
dense in $\alpha_r$, so the nearest other eigenvalue is only $0.019$ away
(separation ratio $0.09$) and 42 roots fall within $10\,\%$ of $|\alpha_{\rm
disc}|$. That is the expected discretisation signature of the continuous spectrum
crowding the discrete mode, hence \emph{acceptable} (topology correct) rather
than \emph{agrees} (which would need a clean Euclidean gap).

A companion row, \texttt{balakumar\_malik1992\_via\_xirenfu}, treats the same
condition purely as a spatial second-mode anchor: pyMack
$\alpha=0.2202103-0.0027906\,\imag$ gives $\alpha_r$ relative error $0.096\,\%$
(essentially exact) and $\alpha_i$ relative error $9.7\,\%$ --- the same
$\sim\!10\,\%$ inter-method $\alpha_i$ spread seen on Malik case~6
(Figure~\ref{fig:bm1992}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{../verification/second_mode/balakumar_malik1992_branches/overlay.png}
\caption{Balakumar \& Malik (1992), $M=4.5$: pyMack's complex-$\alpha$ spectrum

\subsection{Mack (1984) Fig.~10.6 --- second-mode growth vs.\ Reynolds number}
\label{sec:mack106}

\textbf{Reference source.} L.~M. Mack, ``Boundary-layer linear stability
theory'' (1984), AGARD Report R-709, Fig.~10.6.

\textbf{Flow conditions.} Air, adiabatic wall, 2-D wave ($\psi=0^\circ$), at
$M=4.5$, $5.8$, $7$, and $10$. Two modelling choices make the comparison
defensible: the cold \texttt{table\_11\_1} edge state (resolving a historical
$\sim\!6\times$ gap that was an edge-temperature error) and a wall-normal domain
$y_{\max}\approx4\,\delta^\ast/L^\ast$ that grows with Mach ($y_{\max}=40$ at
M4.5 up to $140$ at M10; a fixed short box starves the thick high-Mach boundary
layer).

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The maximum second-mode temporal growth rate
$\omega_{i,\max}$ as a function of $R$ (Mack's $L^\ast$ scale, temporal second
mode $c_r\approx0.9$).

\textbf{Result --- \emph{agrees}/\emph{acceptable}.} Median relative error over
the overlapping $R$-stations: \textbf{M4.5: 1.0\,\%}, \textbf{M7: 2.8\,\%},
\textbf{M10: 4.0\,\%} (all at/below the 5\,\% digitisation floor, verdict
\emph{agrees}); \textbf{M5.8: 6.7\,\%} (a small residual above the floor, verdict
\emph{acceptable}). The family agrees from M4.5 to M10, so the M5.8 residual is
local, \emph{not} a monotonic high-Mach under-prediction. Here the M4.5
and M10 endpoints are shown as representatives (Figures~\ref{fig:mack106_m45}
and \ref{fig:mack106_m100}; the M5.8 and M7 overlays exist under
the \texttt{mack\_fig10\_6\_M58} and \texttt{\_M70} directories and are tabulated

\subsection{Egorov et al.\ (2006) --- Mach 6 flat-plate second mode}
\label{sec:egorov}

\textbf{Reference source.} I.~Egorov, A.~Fedorov \& V.~Soudakov (2006)
\emph{Theor.\ Comput.\ Fluid Dyn.} \textbf{20}:41--54
(DOI 10.1007/s00162-006-0027-9): 2-D DNS second-mode growth over a Mach-6
adiabatic flat plate, reported to agree with LST.

\textbf{Flow conditions.} Perfect gas $\gamma=1.4$, $Pr=0.72$, power-law
viscosity $\mu/\mu_e=(T/T_e)^{0.7}$; $M_\infty=6$, $Re_L=2\times10^6$, adiabatic
wall; mode-S circular frequency $\omega\approx200$ (plate-length units) at the
synchronisation station $x=1$ ($R=\sqrt{2\times10^6}=1414$).

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The second-mode instability band and the
most-amplified dimensionless frequency. \textbf{Honest caveat:} the paper
presents the DNS-vs-LST comparison as a growth-rate-vs-$x$ curve with no printed
growth-rate digit, so no $\le$5\,\% numeric anchor exists; the verdict metric is
the frequency match.

\textbf{Result --- \emph{acceptable}.} pyMack's discrete second mode
($c_r\approx0.92$--$0.94$) is unstable across $\omega_E\in[140,260]$ (bracketing
Egorov's $\omega=200$), most-amplified at $\omega_E\approx215$ ($7.5\,\%$ from
$200$); at exactly $\omega=200$ the spatial growth is
$-\alpha_i=2.90\times10^{-3}$ with $c_r=0.934$. pyMack thus places the M6
second-mode instability squarely in Egorov's stated band with the amplified
frequency essentially on top of their forcing choice (Figure~\ref{fig:egorov}).

\subsection{Ma \& Zhong (2003) Fig.~15 --- M4.5 second-mode neutral curve}
\label{sec:mazhong}

\textbf{Reference source.} X.~Ma \& X.~Zhong (2003) \emph{J.\ Fluid Mech.}
\textbf{488}:31--78, \S6 / Fig.~15: full neutral curves $\omega(R)$ for the
first and second mode at $M=4.5$, with the second-mode branch points quoted
in text at $R=806$ (branch~I) and $R=999.6$ (branch~II) for $F=2.2\times10^{-4}$.

\textbf{Flow conditions.} Air, adiabatic wall, 2-D ($\psi=0^\circ$), $M=4.5$,
$T_e=65.15$~K, unit $Re=7.2\times10^6$/m, Sutherland $S=110.33$~K, $Pr=0.72$,
$\gamma=1.4$; spatial formulation at fixed $F=2.2\times10^{-4}$.

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The full neutral curve $\omega(R)$ traced by
pyMack's $-\alpha_i=0$ contour, anchored against the two second-mode branch
points Ma \& Zhong quote in text at $F=2.2\times10^{-4}$.

\textbf{Result --- \emph{agrees}.} pyMack's second-mode neutral band
reproduces the closed loop of Fig.~15 (nose near $R\approx280$, upper branch
$\omega\approx0.225$, lower branch $\omega\approx0.18$) to $\sim\!3$--$5\,\%$
across the full range. At the quoted $F=2.2\times10^{-4}$ station, pyMack's
band crosses at $R=831$ (branch~I, vs.\ $806$, $3.1\,\%$) and $R=1033.4$
(branch~II, vs.\ $999.6$, $3.4\,\%$) --- both inside the \emph{agrees}
threshold. The first-mode cutoff (upper) branch also tracks Fig.~15 well; the
low-$\omega$ onset (lower) branch is only partially resolved, the same
extraction limitation seen on the \"Ozgen cases below (Figure~\ref{fig:mazhong}).

\subsection{\"Ozgen \& K\i rcal\i\ (2008) Fig.~3 --- first-mode neutral curves}

\textbf{Flow conditions.} Air, adiabatic wall, 2-D ($\psi=0^\circ$); temporal

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The neutral-curve branches $\alpha_L(Re_L)$.

\textbf{Result --- \emph{agrees}/\emph{acceptable}.} After the references were
re-digitised correctly and a discrete-mode extractor (eigenfunction-decay
$+$ $y_{\max}$-stationarity) replaced the earlier $c_r$-band classifier that had
excluded the genuine first mode, the picture is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{M4 --- agrees} (Figure~\ref{fig:ozgen_m4}). Complete two-lobe match:

\item \textbf{M2, M3, M6 --- acceptable} (Figures~\ref{fig:ozgen_m2},

$R$-range by eigenvalue continuation (lower $7\,\%$, upper $8\,\%$); M3 first-mode

\item \textbf{M7, M8, M10 --- acceptable (topology caveat).} The dominant
second-mode cutoff branch agrees ($2$--$6\,\%$), but pyMack predicts a
\emph{connected} first--second-mode band where \"Ozgen shows two lobes separated

\subsection{Mack (1984) Fig.~10.1 --- first-mode neutral loop}
\label{sec:mack101}

\textbf{Reference source.} Mack (1984) AGARD-R-709, Fig.~10.1, the innermost
(Complete-Equations) neutral loop, re-digitised from p.~90 with both branches
plus nose.

\textbf{Flow conditions.} Air, adiabatic wall, 2-D ($\psi=0^\circ$), at
$M=1.6$ and $M=2.2$; temporal neutral loop ($c_i=0$) in $(R, F\!\times\!10^4)$.

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The first-mode neutral loop (lower onset $+$ upper
cutoff branches) and the critical Reynolds number.

\textbf{Result --- \emph{disagrees}.} At \textbf{M1.6}, loop-average median
$|\Delta F|/F = 37\,\%$ (lower $34\,\%$, upper $39\,\%$), critical $R$ pyMack

\subsection{Sharp cone --- Sivasubramanian (2012), Mach 6 second-mode $N$-factor}
\label{sec:cone}

\textbf{Reference source.} P.~Sivasubramanian (2012) PhD thesis, Univ.\ of
Arizona (hdl:10150/228514), Fig.~5.1 --- $N$-factor curves for axisymmetric
waves from a low-amplitude DNS wave-packet simulation on a sharp $7^\circ$ cone
at Mach 6 (BAM6QT); the linear parent of Sivasubramanian \& Fasel (2015)
\emph{J.\ Fluid Mech.} \textbf{768}:175--218. The thesis states verbatim that the
most-amplified axisymmetric wave reaches $N\approx9.5$ at $F=1.071\times10^{-5}$
($f^\ast\approx210$~kHz).

\textbf{Flow conditions.} Sharp $7^\circ$ half-angle cone, $M_\infty=6$,
isothermal $T_w=300$~K ($T_w/T_e=4.70$), axisymmetric ($\psi=0^\circ$).
Taylor--Maccoll edge state $M_e=5.356$, $T_e=63.78$~K, $U_e=857.5$~m/s;
Mangler path (transverse curvature omitted). $N$ integrated over the same
physical extent as thesis Fig.~5.1 (axial $x^\ast=0.30\to0.59$~m, from the lower
neutral point).

\textbf{Quantity compared.} The second-mode peak-$N$ frequency and the peak
linear $N$-factor.

\textbf{Result --- \emph{acceptable}.} \textbf{Frequency: exact.} pyMack's
peak-$N$ frequency is $205$~kHz ($N=8.11$), i.e.\ $2.4\,\%$ from the thesis
$210$~kHz; evaluated \emph{at} $210$~kHz pyMack gives $N=8.05$ --- pyMack
independently reproduces $F=1.071\times10^{-5}$ as the most-amplified frequency.
\textbf{$N$-factor: $\sim\!15\,\%$ short.} pyMack $8.0$--$8.1$ vs.\ thesis $9.5$.
The shape tracks the thesis well through $x^\ast\approx0.50$~m (pyMack $6.5$ vs.\
$\sim\!6.6$), but the self-similar Blasius/Mangler local growth rate decays
faster than the cone DNS in the back half, so the last $\sim\!1.4$ of $N$ is not
accumulated --- the expected signature of omitting transverse curvature and the
real non-similar cone mean flow. The domain-matched $N=8.05$ is bracketed by the
BAM6QT design review (arXiv:2509.10411: $N\!\sim\!8$ at end of cone, $\sim\!9$ at
end of flare, $>\!10$ low-noise). Verdict \emph{acceptable}: frequency agrees
exactly, $N$ sits right at the $15\,\%$ boundary with a bounded, physically
understood deficit, and there is no topology/mode mismatch (Figure~\ref{fig:cone}).

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Across 34 audited cases the repository records
\textbf{7 agrees, 13 acceptable, 8 disagrees, 6 pending}. The headline is clean:
\emph{every second-mode case agrees or is acceptable} --- across Malik (1990),
Balakumar \& Malik (1992), Ma \& Zhong (2003), Mack (1984) Fig.~10.6, Egorov et
al.\ (2006), the Sivasubramanian (2012) cone, and the collaborator's Mach 5.35
code. The base of the pyramid (Orszag, Malik) is essentially exact; the
second-mode design target is validated from Mach 4.5 to 10 and on a flight cone.

First-mode results are split by extraction path: \"Ozgen's cleanly-resolvable
discrete mode \emph{agrees}/is \emph{acceptable} across M2--M6 (M4 to the
digitisation floor), with the low-Mach onset continuous-spectrum-limited
rather than physically wrong; the Mack Fig.~10.1/10.3/10.4 path
\emph{disagrees} at increasing Mach --- a documented under-amplification /
too-high critical Reynolds number, computed through a separate solver path
whose Fig.~10.4 magnitudes are explicitly lower-confidence pending the same
discrete-mode scrutiny already applied to the \"Ozgen cases. Used within
these bounds --- trust the second mode; treat the first mode as qualitative
at high Mach --- pyMack is a well-characterised tool.
